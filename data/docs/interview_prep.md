# Interview Preparation Notes - Core CS Quick Reference

## DSA priorities for service and product companies

For eight weeks of preparation, spend the first three weeks on arrays, strings,
hashing, and two pointers; weeks four and five on linked lists, stacks, queues,
and binary trees; weeks six and seven on recursion, backtracking, and dynamic
programming; and the final week on graphs and revision. Target 150 to 200
solved problems overall, with at least 40 at medium difficulty.

## SQL essentials

Interviewers most commonly test joins (inner versus left), GROUP BY with
HAVING, window functions such as ROW_NUMBER and RANK, and finding the
second-highest salary. Practice writing queries by hand without an editor,
because many online tests disable autocomplete.

## REST API questions

Be ready to explain the difference between PUT and PATCH, what idempotency
means, common status codes (200, 201, 400, 401, 404, 500), and how token-based
authentication works end to end. If you have used FastAPI, be prepared to
explain dependency injection and request validation with Pydantic models.

## Machine learning basics for intern roles

For non-research roles, interviewers expect you to explain train-test splits,
overfitting versus underfitting, precision versus recall, and why you chose a
specific model in your project. Be ready to state your project's dataset size,
feature count, and evaluation metric from memory.

## Behavioral round

Prepare three stories using the STAR format: one about debugging a hard
problem, one about a team conflict or disagreement, and one about delivering
under a deadline. Keep each story under two minutes. Always end with what you
measured or learned.

## Common mistakes

The three most common reasons interns fail interviews are: claiming skills on
the resume they cannot demonstrate, jumping to code before clarifying the
problem, and not testing edge cases aloud. Practice narrating your thought
process while solving at least ten problems.
